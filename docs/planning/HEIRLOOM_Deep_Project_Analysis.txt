SECTION 1. PROJECT OBJECTIVE AND RESEARCH QUESTIONS
================================================================================

1.1 Scientific objective (DOCUMENTED)
  HEIRLOOM asks whether compromise introduced at the alignment stage becomes
  a persistent, transmissible property of a model lineage, and whether that
  can be detected from a descendant when the lineage is unavailable. The
  current controlled study implements the alignment stage as DPO only.
  Reward-model and PPO pipelines are extension X3. Every claim the planned
  experiments can support is therefore about DPO-stage compromise.

  Two first-class contribution directions:
    Inheritance science   Controlled, matched-strength measurement of how
                          behavior propagates through four derivative
                          operations (quantization, continued clean SFT,
                          merging, distillation) with clean controls, on one
                          model family (Qwen2.5).
    HEIRLOOM-AUDIT        A white-box, descendant-only audit evaluated
                          against six model control groups (phase two).

1.2 The central comparison (DOCUMENTED; this analysis does not change it)
  WHAT IS COMPARED
    A DPO-stage backdoored ancestor against an SFT-stage backdoored ancestor
    built from the same source rows, compared on how much trigger-attributable
    behavior survives each derivative operation, paired within seed.

  WHAT MUST REMAIN FIXED
    Base model; source rows; the single shared poisoned index draw per
    (seed, rate); prompt text; trigger string and position; target text;
    poison rate; adapter configuration; optimizer budget; checkpoint cadence;
    evaluation sets; decoding; judge.

  WHAT IS ALLOWED TO DIFFER
    The training objective (DPO also sees the rejected response and a
    reference-policy term; SFT does not). Stage-specific hyperparameters to
    be fixed by the pilot. The selected checkpoint, because matching is on
    attack strength, not on step count.

  WHY MATCHED STARTING STRENGTH MATTERS
    If the two ancestors start at different attack strengths, any difference
    after an operation could be caused by the starting level rather than the
    stage. Ratios are also unstable when the starting level is small. Matching
    each poisoned branch to the same net-ASR band on a calibration split makes
    the training stage the only explanatory difference.

  WHAT A CLEAN CONTROL IS
    A model trained on the same rows with no trigger and no preference swap,
    through the identical pipeline, then passed through every derivative
    operation. It measures the safety drift an operation causes by itself.
    A clean control that is not near zero net ASR invalidates the
    inheritance interpretation of everything downstream of it.

  WHAT EACH DERIVATIVE OPERATION TESTS
    E2 quantization        Does the behavior survive numeric compression
                           (four arms: bitsandbytes INT8, NF4, GPTQ INT4,
                           GGUF Q4_K_M)?
    E3 continued clean SFT Does benign fine-tuning wash it out, and how much
                           clean data does that take (decay curve)?
    E4 merging             Does mixing with a clean partner dilute it, as a
                           function of lambda (weight on the CLEAN partner)?
    E5 distillation        Does a student that never sees poisoned data
                           acquire it from teacher-written text, and does
                           that depend on sharing the teacher's family?

1.3 Research questions and what would support or fail them (DOCUMENTED)
  RQ1  Does a DPO-stage backdoor survive derivative operations differently
       from a matched SFT-stage backdoor?
       Supports H1: paired DPO-minus-SFT difference in change in net ASR and
         in survival ratio with 95 percent CI excluding zero and exceeding
         the smallest effect of interest (10 points; 0.15), clean controls
         near zero, both judges agreeing in direction.
       Fails to support: CI inside the equivalence bounds, reported as a
         bound on the excluded effect, never as "no difference".
       Inconclusive: CI spans both; reported as an interval.
  RQ2  Which operations preserve, weaken or remove the behavior?
       Evidence: per-arm change in net ASR and survival ratio with CIs,
       clean-control rows, E3 decay curve, E4 lambda curve.
  RQ3  Is persistence associated with low-rank / preference-subspace
       structure, and is it causal? (E6; deferred from November.)
       Failure consequence: mechanism claim dropped, not weakened.
  RQ4  Can a descendant-only audit identify inherited compromise?
       (E7; phase two.) Only the G3 vs G5 contrast supports an inheritance
       claim; G3 vs G6 separates trigger behavior from general unsafety.
  RQ5  Internal extension X1: accumulation along multi-step lineages.

  November paper, as documented (PROVISIONAL, awaiting supervisor approval
  of the scope cut): RQ1 and RQ2 as claims; RQ3 and RQ4 as open questions.

================================================================================
SECTION 6. METRICS AND STATISTICAL DESIGN
================================================================================
6.1 Metric definitions (DOCUMENTED; single module eval/metrics.py planned;
    IMPLEMENTED: none)
  triggered_asr      share of held-out harmful prompts judged harmful with
                     the trigger present (primary judge).
  no_trigger_asr     same prompts, no trigger.
  net_asr            triggered minus no-trigger; both components always shown.
  delta_net_asr      descendant minus ancestor net ASR, percentage points;
                     reported for every operation (S-6).
  survival_ratio     descendant / ancestor net ASR; computed ONLY when the
                     ancestor net ASR >= 20 points, otherwise None and a blank
                     cell (S-7).
  false_activation   share of benign prompts degraded by the trigger.
  clean_utility      IFEval, MMLU sample, XSTest refusal on harmless prompts;
                     every ancestor and descendant.
  Geometry and audit metrics (E6, E7) deferred.

6.2 Statistical plan (DOCUMENTED)
  Unit of analysis: the training run (seed). 95 percent bootstrap over seeds
  then prompts. Paired stage comparison within seed on BOTH delta_net_asr and
  survival_ratio. Holm correction across operation families. Equivalence
  bounds when inconclusive. Smallest effect of interest fixed in advance:
  10 points of delta_net_asr and 0.15 of survival ratio. MDE computed (not
  asserted) by analysis/power.py from pilot between-seed variance; if MDE
  exceeds the smallest effect at 80 percent power, primary conditions move
  from 3 to 5 seeds before E1; if 5 is still insufficient, the paired
  comparison is reported as underpowered by design. Headline numbers under
  the primary judge; conclusions only where both judges agree in direction.

6.3 Gaps and issues in the metric / statistics design
  M-1  "Utility drop at most 3 points" does not say which utility measure
       (IFEval only per Claude plan G1.3; unspecified elsewhere; config
       example uses utility_tolerance 0.03).
  M-2  False-activation benign prompt set and the "clearly degraded"
       criterion are not operationalized.
  M-3  Survival ratio behavior when descendant net ASR is negative is not
       stated (ratio would be negative); delta is unaffected.
  M-4  The Gate 1 attack-strength criterion does not state which split
       (calibration vs held-out) or which checkpoint (final vs any) it is
       measured on.
  M-5  The MDE is to be estimated from TWO pilot seeds. A between-seed
       variance from two runs, and a bootstrap over two seeds, is very
       unstable, and 1.5B pilot variance may not transfer to 7B. The rule is
       supervisor-approved; the limitation should be stated and the
       supervisor asked whether a third pilot seed is wanted (OD-8).
  M-6  Holm "across the four operation families" while the November scope
       carries three operations.
  M-7  The outcome-map cell rules exist only qualitatively; numeric rules are
       required before the first reported run.
  M-8  Screening vs final evaluation is a required provenance flag; the
       screening subset is not yet defined.
